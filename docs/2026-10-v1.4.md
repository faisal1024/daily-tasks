# 1.4 (October 2026)

## Lock and unlock

Setting the day is one tap on Today. Changing a set day takes a deliberate
step: it is unlocked in Settings, nowhere else.

**Locking**

- "Set today" stays on Today's status line (and "Set these three" in Ideas when
  the three are picked). Auto-set still sets a day with at least one task at the
  chosen time (noon by default).

**While today is set**

- Today shows a quiet locked state under the card: a lock glyph, the status
  ("Today is set. 2 to go." or "Set automatically at 12:00 PM. 2 to go.") and
  the line "Unlock in Settings". It is one VoiceOver element that reads the
  status and whose hint is "Unlock in Settings". There is no Change button,
  link or alert on Today.
- Wording: the state is always "Today is set" (never "Today is locked");
  "Unlock" and "Keep locked" are the verbs.
- Times follow the device's 12 or 24-hour setting ("12:00 PM" or "12:00"),
  through the same helper as the time pickers in Settings (`formatTime`), so
  the status line on Today and the unlock confirm in Settings always agree
  (`formatClockTime`; "12:00 PM" style if the device's format can't be had).
  The line stays on a finished three-of-three set day too, so the lock always
  shows.
- A tap where a change used to be (a row's words when its menu is empty, an
  empty slot) shows a short note with a lock, also spoken to VoiceOver: "Today
  is set. Unlock it in Settings to change your three." A row that still has
  a menu (Break it down, timer choices) shows the same words as the menu's
  message.
- Not allowed: adding (typed, Ideas, saved items, routines, last night's draft,
  yesterday's leftovers, brain dump), removing, editing a task's words, and
  "Not today". The store's reducer refuses each of these on a set day
  (`addTask`, `addTasks`, `editTask`, `deleteTask`, `notToday`, and through
  them `addParkedTask`, `addRoutineToToday`, `applyTomorrowDraft`,
  `resolveRollover`), so the rule does not depend on the screen.
- Still allowed: ticking and unticking, timers, break-down steps (add, tick,
  clear), the evening check-in. The widget, Live Activity, Siri and
  notifications are unchanged: they tick and start timers, and never unlock.
- Blocked copy points to Settings: "Today is set. Unlock it in Settings to add
  one." (routines in Ideas, and the saved-for-later sheet).
- Nothing new that was typed is dropped when the day is set meanwhile
  (auto-set can fire mid-typing). Words in an open slot, and every pick of a
  brain dump under way (the first-run one too), are saved for later with a
  note: "Today is set. 3 saved for later in Ideas." The brain-dump sheet
  stays open and says so at each step: while writing, "Today is set, so
  everything is saved in Ideas."; at the review, "Today is set, so these are
  saved in Ideas for another day." over a plain list (nothing to tick, each
  row reads "<item>, saved for later") and the button "Save all for later".
- An edit to an existing task's words that is still open when the day is
  set (a tap on "Set today" with the keyboard up, or auto-set at its time)
  is saved (1.4.1; before, the field closed and the words were dropped).
  The row reports the words being typed while they differ from the saved
  ones (`TaskRow` `onDraftChange`, the store's `noteOpenEdit`), and the
  store's `setDay` saves them with `editTask` just before it dispatches
  `lockToday` / `autoLockToday`, so the reducer still refuses every edit on
  a set day. Only when that dispatch really sets the day: an auto-set that
  is refused (the day was unlocked in Settings) saves nothing mid-typing.
  Then the field closes, showing the saved words. Saved rather than kept
  open: the lock forbids starting a change to a set day, not finishing one
  begun before it was set, and a field left open on a set day would be a
  way round the lock.
- A set day's "Up next" row has no empty line under its words when there is
  nothing to offer there (no "Not today", and no "Break it down").

**Unlocking**

- Settings shows an "Unlock today" row only while today is set. It is the
  first card on the screen, above every section.
- Tapping it asks "Unlock today?" with "Keep locked" (the prominent,
  cancel-style button) and "Unlock" (plain, not red). The message depends on
  how the day was set; the rule is the same for both:
  - set by hand: "Your three will be open to change again. You can set today
    again any time."
  - set automatically: "The app set today automatically at 12:00 PM. Unlocking
    keeps it open for the rest of today."
- After "Unlock", a short note "Today is unlocked." shows where the row was
  (and is spoken to VoiceOver).
- `unlockToday` returns whether it unlocked anything. If the confirm was left
  open past midnight, the new day isn't set, so nothing is tracked, shown or
  spoken.
- An explicit unlock holds for the rest of that day: auto-set does not re-lock
  it, even when its time has already passed (`manualUnlockDate`, saved with the
  state and cleared at rollover). A manual "Set today" locks it again, and it
  can be unlocked again the same way.
- Analytics: `day_unlocked` with `source: "settings"`, only for an unlock that
  really happened.

## Milestone suggestions

Owner's request: "Some of the suggestions should be based on my long-term
milestones. It should look at the next milestone and suggest something off of
that."

When there is a goal path with a milestone not yet reached, the Ideas sheet
shows a group **"Next on your path"** with the milestone's title and one or
two suggestions. It sits below the source chip and the hint, above the
general ideas, which are then headed **"More ideas"**. Free users get built-in suggestions (no AI, nothing leaves the
device); Plus users get AI ones.

Tone as everywhere: calm, no guilt, no hype. A suggestion that isn't picked
shows nothing. Nothing is marked missed or overdue.

### Which milestone

`nextIncompleteMilestone(milestones, completedMilestoneIds)`
(`lib/daily-tasks/milestones.ts`): the first one not reached. Reaching it
moves the group to the next one. No group when there is no path, when every
milestone is reached, or in the "Saved for later" view of the sheet.

### Built-in suggestions (free, and the fallback for Plus)

`lib/daily-tasks/milestone-ideas.ts` (pure, no react-native imports):

- `localMilestoneIdeas(milestone, today, lead?)` returns the four built-in
  lines in an order picked by the day and the milestone id, so the pair
  changes from day to day but not within a day:
  - `<title>: 10 easy minutes` (10 min)
  - `<title>: do the first small step` (5 min)
  - `<title>: 15 focused minutes` (15 min)
  - `<title>: plan the next small step` (5 min)
- The title leads and a small action follows, so the line reads as a task.
  Each line's time estimate matches what it says.
- On a starter path (`pathSource` "template": "Start small", "Repeat the
  rhythm", "Grow the challenge") the lines open with the goal's title
  instead, e.g. `Run a 5K: 10 easy minutes`.
- A task is at most 64 characters, so a long title is cut at a word and ends
  with "…" (`shortMilestoneTitle`), never through an emoji or an accented
  letter (`cutGraphemes`). Quote marks and closing punctuation are removed
  (including "…" and the full-width 。！？、).
- A title that already names a length of time (a digit followed by min,
  minute(s), hour(s) or hr, as in "Run for 10 minutes") gets the two lines
  without a number first, so it never reads "Run for 10 minutes: 10 easy
  minutes".
- Only the title is used. The description is sent to the AI (below) but not
  put into the built-in lines.

### AI suggestions (Plus)

The plan request (same route, same auth) now carries the next milestone, and
the response marks the suggestions made for it. Field details are in
`docs/momentum-ai-proxy.md` ("Next milestone").

- `requestMomentumAiPlan` sends `nextMilestone: { title, description? }`.
  Not with a fresh-path request, and not while the path is the starter one
  (`milestoneToAskAbout`): the AI's first plan replaces that path.
- Suggestions the proxy returns with `forMilestone: true` are saved with a
  `milestoneKey` (milestone id plus its title, `milestoneKey()`). The title is
  part of the key because ids are positional: after an edit or a new path the
  same id can name a different step, and an old suggestion must stop counting.
- When the plan is stored, a mark is kept only if it names the first open
  milestone of the path as stored (`settleMilestoneMarks`), so a path that
  was replaced or edited while the request was out never shows ideas made
  for another step.
- `buildMilestoneIdeaGroup` picks the group: up to two AI suggestions whose
  key matches the next open milestone (`source: "ai"`); otherwise up to two
  built-in ones (`source: "local"`).

The built-in ones show whenever there are no matching AI suggestions: a free
user, an older proxy that sends no mark, the model marking none, a failed AI
request (the app keeps or rebuilds the plan as before), a milestone just
reached, or a path edited since the plan was made.

### Rules in the sheet

- Same one-tap add as any other idea. Disabled when today is full or set. A
  suggestion already on today's list shows as added.
- An AI suggestion shown in the group is not listed again under the general
  ideas, and "Add all" only covers the general ideas (shown when two or more
  of them can still be added). With no general ideas left, there is no "More
  ideas" heading and no "Add all".
- A tapped suggestion whose words are already on today is refused by the
  store (`addSuggestions`), so a double tap adds once. Typing the same words
  by hand is still allowed.
- A built-in suggestion with the same words as a general idea or a routine due
  today is skipped, and the next built-in line takes its place.
- Accessibility: the group title is a header read as "Next on your path:
  <title>", said once; each row is a button labelled "Add <text>" (or
  "<text>, added") with nothing more about the milestone. "More ideas" is a
  header too.

### Today, midday

"Next on your path: <milestone>" still opens Progress. While there is room to
add (today isn't set or full), a second link under it, "Get ideas for this",
opens Ideas scrolled to the group. The link is hidden once every idea in the
group is already on today.

### Analytics

`milestone_idea_added` with `source: "ai" | "local"`, sent when a suggestion
from the group lands on today (the store says so; a refused or repeated tap
is not counted). The milestone and task text are never sent.

### Server

`server/providers/plan-contract.mjs` changed (prompt, schema, validation,
sanitising). The Worker needs `cd server && npx wrangler@4 deploy` after
merge. Until then the new app shows the built-in suggestions, and older apps
are unaffected either way.

## Task-aware notifications

Reminders used to say the same fixed lines every day ("Today's Three still
need a little attention."). From 1.4 they say what the tasks are. Owner's
decisions: free for everyone; written on the phone (no AI, nothing sent
anywhere); task names shown by default, with a switch to hide them; the same
number of reminders at the same times as before.

Tone is unchanged: calm, no guilt, no red. Something unfinished is "still
open", never "missed" or "overdue".

### What they say

`lib/daily-tasks/reminder-copy.ts` is a pure builder:
`buildReminderCopy({ kind, dayKey, slotIndex, tasks, locked, yesterdayOpen, hideNames, certain, evening })`
returns `{ title, body, taskId? }`, or `null` when there is nothing to remind
about. It sends nothing anywhere.

The title changes with the situation. Often the title is the task and the
body is the situation; other variants keep the slot's own title ("A calm
start", "A small nudge", "Still time today") and put the task first in the
body.

| Situation | Title | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None done | Call the dentist | First up today. Five minutes is a start. |
| None done, one task only | A small nudge | Today's one thing: Call the dentist. Five minutes is a start. |
| Some done, more than one open | Next: Write the report | 1 of 3 done. |
| Some done, one open | One to go: Walk | A small finish is still a finish. |
| The next reminder, in the evening | Still time today | Still open tonight: Walk. Five minutes still counts. |
| Written ahead (see below) | Write the report | Still open? A small start counts. |
| Written ahead, whole list | A small nudge | Today's three: Call the dentist · Write the report · Walk. Anything still open? |
| Written ahead, evening | Still time today | Walk. Still open tonight? Five minutes still counts. |
| Nothing set, one open from yesterday | Still open from yesterday | Renew passport. Want it in today's three? |
| Nothing set, several open from yesterday | A calm start | 2 still open from yesterday: Renew passport and Buy stamps. Want them in today's three? |
| Tomorrow morning, written today | A calm start | From yesterday: Walk. Still open? It could be one of today's three. |
| Nothing set, nothing left over | A calm start | Nothing picked yet. What are today's three? |
| Everything done | no reminder (as before) | |
| "Hide task names" on, something open | the slot's general title | a name-free line, the next of four each time: by day "Today's three are waiting when you are." / "A small start still counts." / "Five minutes is a start." / "Pick one of today's three and begin anywhere."; in the evening "A small finish is still a finish." / "No rush. Five minutes still counts." / "Still time for one of today's three." / "Today's three are here if there's time tonight." |
| "Hide task names" on, nothing set | the slot's general title | "Nothing picked yet. What are today's three?" and its variants (yesterday's tasks are not named) |

- **The named task rotates.** The next reminder names the next task (the
  first still open, in Today's order). Each later one today names
  `open[slotIndex % open.length]`, so a day with three open tasks walks
  through all three. "From yesterday" lines take yesterday's open tasks in
  turn too, and some variants say the whole list.
- **Variants go by the slot's place in the whole day** (`reminderVariantIndex`:
  10:00 is the first, 17:00 the fifth; kinds are not counted apart), each
  one step on from the one before, so two reminders in a row never share a
  body, and a reschedule doesn't reshuffle them. The pools that have a
  whole-list line keep it at the same index, so two in a row never both read
  out the list.
- **A one-task day is never "first up"**: "Today's one thing: …" or "Just
  one today: …".
- **Evening slots (17:00 on) have evening wording**, also the progress
  reminders that "Every hour" sends into the evening.
- "Today's three" is only said of three tasks; otherwise "On today's list".
  Names that share a line are cut shorter (28 characters).
- Task text is made safe first (`reminderTaskName`): newlines, tabs, control
  characters and text-direction overrides become spaces, runs of spaces
  collapse, and anything longer than 60 visible characters is cut with an
  ellipsis. Cutting counts whole characters (an emoji, a flag, an accented
  letter), using `Intl.Segmenter` where it exists and a built-in fallback on
  Hermes. A name that already ends in ".", "!", "?" or "…" gets no second
  full stop. The scheduler cleans every plan's title and body the same way
  (`cleanReminderText` in `fitPlanToLimit`: 90 and 240 characters), so
  another strategy's words are safe too.
- "Still open from yesterday" comes from the tasks waiting on Today's
  "from yesterday" card (`yesterdayOpenTexts`). Ones already carried into
  today or let go are not mentioned, and neither is anything older than
  yesterday.
- The morning nudge on a day with a draft from the evening close still reads
  "Your three for Tuesday: A · B · C" (general wording when names are
  hidden). The draft's names are cleaned and capped like any other.
- A set (locked) day with nothing on it gets the general line: nothing can
  be added, so nothing is offered.

### When they are rewritten

iOS cannot run the app's code when a local notification is delivered, so
what a reminder says is fixed when it is scheduled. The store therefore
rewrites the pending reminders:

- when the app opens, and every time it comes to the front;
- when a task is added, ticked, unticked, removed, reordered or reworded;
- when the day is set or reopened, when the "from yesterday" card changes,
  and when the day changes;
- when a reminder setting or the notification permission changes.

Changes settle for 500 ms first (`REMINDER_SYNC_DEBOUNCE_MS`), so ticking
two tasks in a row or dragging costs one rewrite. If the app leaves the
front while a rewrite is waiting, it runs at once (a timer may never fire in
the background). A rewrite that fails is forgotten rather than taken as
done, so the next trigger asks for it again.

**How a rewrite is applied** (`runPlan` in `notifications.ts`): the plan is
scheduled first, then the owned reminders that are no longer in it are
cancelled. Ids are fixed by day, kind and time, and scheduling an id that is
already pending replaces it, so there is never a moment with nothing
pending; an interrupted rewrite leaves the old reminders, not none. A
pending reminder that already has the same title, body and time is left
alone, so a sync that changes nothing writes nothing. Its time is read from
its own `data.fireAt` (ms), which every reminder carries: on iOS a pending
one-off's date cannot be read back, because expo-notifications schedules it
as a time interval and reports `{ type: "timeInterval", seconds, repeats }`
(the seconds counted from when it was scheduled). The trigger is the
fallback (Android's timestamp); a reminder with neither, such as one
scheduled by an earlier build, is rescheduled once. One failed schedule or cancel does not stop the rest (a failed
replacement removes the out-of-date one it was meant to replace). Only when
old and new together would pass iOS's limit are the old ones cancelled
first.

**Reminders that already went off** go stale in Notification Center (a
count that has moved on, a task since done). A rewrite clears the owned ones
that are showing whenever the app is in front, and when "Hide task names"
has just been turned on. Other notifications are never dismissed.

Each rewrite also schedules one morning nudge for each of the next six days
(as before). Tomorrow's is written from what is still open today, so it is
right even if the app isn't opened first: "From yesterday: Call the
dentist. Still open? It could be one of today's three.". "Yesterday" is
relative to the day it arrives. The days after that have nothing to go on
and ask plainly.

Times are unchanged: an empty day gets hourly morning nudges (8:00 to 12:00
on weekdays, 10:00 to 12:00 at weekends); a day with tasks gets 10:00,
12:00, 14:00, 16:00 and hourly 17:00 to 22:00 (or hourly 8:00 to 21:00 with
"Every hour"). All in the phone's local time; the next days' nudges use
calendar arithmetic, so a clock change keeps the local hour.

**When the store's day and the clock's day differ** (`reminderCopyContext`):

- Just after midnight the store can still be on the day before. Its tasks
  are then yesterday's: the new day is planned as an empty one, with the
  open ones "from yesterday". A store two or more days behind says nothing
  about its tasks.
- After flying west the store's day can be the clock's tomorrow. The rest
  of the clock's day still names its tasks, that day's own morning nudge is
  written from them (in the written-ahead wording; none if they are all
  done), and the morning after it gets them as "from yesterday".

Later: reminders are scheduled for an absolute moment (a date trigger), so
after a time-zone change the already pending ones arrive at the old zone's
hour until the app is next opened. Time-zone-following calendar triggers
would fix that; left for a later release.

### The widget-tick limitation

A task can be ticked from the widget without the app running, and the app
only learns of it when it next opens. A reminder scheduled before that tick
cannot know about it. So only the **next** reminder states the count or
says outright that something is open ("1 of 3 done", "One to go"); every
later one today, and tomorrow morning's, is task-first, present tense and
true either way: no counts, and "Walk. Still open? Five minutes is a
start.". Opening the app rewrites them all from the real state.

**The last tick.** When a tick made outside the app leaves nothing open
(the widget's `ToggleTaskIntent`, the Live Activity's Done in
`FocusActions.done`), the native code removes the app's pending reminders
for the rest of today, and only those (ids starting `daily-tasks:<today>:`;
`FocusGroup.clearRemindersIfAllDone` in `FocusActivityShared.swift`).
Tomorrow's morning nudge stays: the widget cannot write another, and the
app may not be opened before it is due. It was written from what was open
today, so it is the task-first kind that is true either way. The app
schedules them all afresh when it next opens. Unticking on the widget afterwards does not bring them back until
then. This needs a check on a device: the widget's intent runs in the
widget extension, and it must be confirmed there that it reaches the app's
pending notifications.

What this still cannot fix: the next reminder (the plain one) may name a
task that was ticked on the widget since, if other tasks are still open.

### The 64 limit

iOS keeps at most 64 pending local notifications per app and silently drops
the rest. The reminders use 20 at most (14 with "Every hour" plus six
mornings; 16 by default). The scheduler still enforces it for any plan
(`fitPlanToLimit`): it counts the pending notifications that are not its
own (the focus timer's end, the trial reminder), always leaves room for at
least two of those, and schedules only the soonest reminders that fit. In
development it warns (with a count, never the words) when a plan has an id
outside the prefix, which is dropped.

The scheduler only ever schedules, cancels or dismisses notifications whose
id starts with `daily-tasks:` (`MANAGED_REMINDER_PREFIX`). The focus timer
(`three-today:focus-timer`), the trial reminder (`plus-trial-ending`) and
their categories are never touched. Tapping a reminder opens Today, as
before. A reminder that names one task carries `data: { taskId, kind }`
(the id, never the text).

### The privacy switch

Settings › Reminders › What reminders say › **Hide task names in
notifications** (off by default): "Reminders and the timer's alert won't
show what your tasks are." (No lock-screen promise: the Live Activity and
the widget still show the task there.) With it on:

- every reminder uses the general titles over lines that name no task,
  including the morning nudge after an evening close; no title or body has
  task text. The lines take turns by slot so two in a row never read the
  same (`NAME_FREE_REMINDER_BODIES`);
- the focus timer's end notification is titled "Time's up" instead of the
  task ("5 more minutes, or mark it done?"), and flipping the switch
  reschedules a running session's;
- reminders already showing (which may name tasks) are cleared when it is
  turned on.

Not covered: the Live Activity and the widget show the task by design.
After "Keep going" on a starter's Live Activity the native side rewrites
the body as a timer's and keeps the title. Under the hidden title ("Time's
up") it writes the hidden body ("5 more minutes, or mark it done?"), the
same as the app, so it never says "Time's up" twice; no task text.

Stored as `notifications.hideTaskNames`; only a saved `true` turns it on, so
older saves and damaged values load as off.

Each reminder switch in Settings is one element to VoiceOver: the switch,
with the row's title as its label, the help text as its hint and its on/off
state. The visible text is hidden from VoiceOver so nothing is read twice.

The standard reminders are local notifications: written and scheduled on
the phone, with no task text sent anywhere to make them. No log line or
analytics event carries reminder text (ids and counts only).

### Plans and strategies

`lib/daily-tasks/reminder-plan.ts` separates what to schedule from
scheduling it:

```ts
interface ReminderPlanItem {
  id: string; // starts with "daily-tasks:"
  fireAt: Date;
  title: string;
  body: string;
  data?: Record<string, unknown>; // ids only, never task text
  kind?: string;
  interruptionLevel?: "passive" | "active" | "timeSensitive" | "critical";
  sound?: boolean | string;
}
type ReminderPlan = ReminderPlanItem[];
type ReminderStrategy = (context: ReminderContext) => ReminderPlan | Promise<ReminderPlan>;
```

- `taskAwareStrategy` is the standard one (the reminders above). It sends
  nothing. `ReminderContext` itself makes no such promise: a strategy that
  sends something must say so itself.
- `morningsStrategy` is the upcoming mornings alone, and
  `combineStrategies(morningsStrategy, another)` joins plans so the mornings
  and another strategy's slots coexist (the earlier strategy wins an id; if
  one fails the whole rewrite fails and is retried).
- A strategy may be async. The context carries `pending` (the owned
  reminders pending now, soonest first, with their time and words) and
  `lastDeliveredAt` (when one last went off, as far as is known). A
  strategy with times of its own choosing (random ones, at most one an
  hour) returns the pending ones it wants to keep with the same id, time
  and words, and the scheduler leaves them as they are instead of picking
  again on every sync.
- `syncNotifications({ ...context, strategy })` plans with a strategy and
  applies the result; `applyReminderPlan(plan)` applies any plan directly.
  One sync at a time. Every item's words are cleaned and the plan is cut to
  the 64 limit, whichever strategy wrote it.

## Reorder

Owner's request: "One task is the one we are working on; it looks a bit
different, but I have no way to change it. I want to reorder so I can pick
which one I am working on next."

Two ways to do it, both on Today, both allowed while today is set (the order
changes, the plan doesn't):

- **Drag.** With two or more open tasks, each open row has a small handle at
  its end (≡). Touch it and move up or down: the row lifts straight away, no
  holding. The other rows make room as you go; let go to drop. A tap on the
  handle opens the row's menu.
- **"Do this next".** In the menu of every open task that isn't first (tap
  the row's words or its handle; long press while its timer runs). It moves
  that task to the front of the open ones.
- **"Move up" / "Move down".** In that menu too, but only where there is no
  room for the handle: a window narrower than 360 points, or text at 1.5× or
  larger.

The first time two or more tasks are open, one muted line under the card says
so: "Drag ≡ to reorder" (without the handle: "To reorder, open a task's menu
and choose Move up or Move down"). It stays for that
visit, goes once something is reordered, and never comes back
(`reorderHintSeen` in the saved state, like `hasSeenOnboarding`).

### The ordering rule

Today's order is the order of `state.tasks`. "Next" is the first open (not
done) task in it. The rule, in `lib/daily-tasks/reorder.ts`:

- Only open tasks move, and only among themselves.
- A done task keeps its slot: its index in the list never changes.
- The open slots are the indexes the open tasks hold, top to bottom. A move
  takes the task out of the open tasks' sequence, puts it back at `toIndex`
  (counting open tasks only; 0 = next; clamped to what exists) and writes the
  sequence back into the same slots.
- Nothing to do gives back the same list (and the same store state): an
  unknown or done task, fewer than two open tasks, or a task already there.

Examples (✓ = done):

| Before          | Move                 | After           |
| --------------- | -------------------- | --------------- |
| A, B, C         | C next               | C, A, B         |
| A, B, C         | A to open index 2    | B, C, A         |
| A, B✓, C        | C next               | C, B✓, A        |
| A✓, B, C        | C next               | A✓, C, B        |
| A, B✓, C, (D)   | B next (it's done)   | unchanged       |

Rows differ in height, so a done row can shift on screen when the rows around
it swap, but it is still the same slot (first, second or third).

### Store

- `moveTask(taskId, toIndex)` and `makeTaskNext(taskId)` (the same as
  `moveTask(taskId, 0)`), reducer actions `moveTask` / `makeTaskNext`.
- They work on a set day. Nothing else about the day changes: completions,
  steps, the "carried over" mark, the lock and its source stay as they are.
- Saved like any task change: the state is persisted, today's history record
  is rewritten in the new order, and the widget's snapshot is rewritten (it
  depends on `state.tasks`).

### What follows the new order

Everything that says "next" reads the first open task from the list each
time, so nothing had to be told about a move:

- Today's "Up next" row, its "Break it down" / "Not today" line, and the
  coach's note (`nextOpenTask`; AI lines are kept per task text, so a move
  never spends another call).
- The widget (`snapshot.nextOpen`) and its start button, Siri's "start my next
  task" and the `dailytasks://focus/start?task=next` link (`focusStartPlan`).

The focus session is tied to its task's id, not a position: a running, paused
or ended timer stays on its task when the order changes, whether that task
moved or another one became next. The Live Activity shows the timed task, so
it doesn't change either. The timed task can be dragged like any other.

### On Today

- The handle is hidden on done rows, while a row's text is being edited, when
  there is only one open task, and where there is no room for it (see above).
  It is the last thing in the row: three lines, 18 points, the muted colour
  at 80% (quieter than the timer beside it, still at least 3:1 on the card).
  It is 26 by 30 points on screen, pulled into the row's gaps so the words
  lose as little width as possible, and no taller than the circle, so rows
  don't grow at any text size.
- Its touch target is 44 by 44 points. That comes from the handle view's own
  hit slop (7 above and below, 6 before, 12 after), which is what widens the
  target on iOS; the gestures carry the same slop, so a touch in that margin
  isn't failed as outside the view (and for Android).
- Picking a row up saves any task text being edited first (the keyboard is
  dismissed, and the field's blur commits it): the drop mounts every row anew,
  which would otherwise lose the edit.
- Moving the finger 6 points up or down on the handle picks the row up. The
  page's scroll view (gesture-handler's `ScrollView`) is told to wait for the
  handle's gesture, so on the handle the drag always wins; everywhere else
  the page scrolls as before. Moving 12 points sideways first leaves the
  touch to the row's swipe (Not today / Delete). The row's tap and long press
  are unchanged. While a row is held the page doesn't scroll, and a row that
  was swiped open closes.
- Haptics: a light tap on pick-up, a selection tick each time the held row
  passes a place, and a light tap on a drop that changed the order (none for
  a drop back where it started).
- The row lifts a little the moment the handle is touched. The dragged row is
  a copy that follows the finger (the same look, nothing live: no swipe, no
  ticking clock). On release it settles in 180 ms. With Reduce Motion the
  lift is instant, there is no settle and the rows don't slide: they are just
  in their new places.
- The drag stays within the task rows. If the list changes under a drag (a
  tick from the widget, midnight), the row goes back where it was. A touch on
  a handle while a drop is still settling does nothing.
- The hero ("Up next") styling changes just after the drop, not while
  dragging.

### How the drag works (and why)

The rows keep their order in the React tree for the whole drag. Reordering
them mid-drag made Fabric take the dragged row's native view out and put it
back at its new index, and iOS cancels a touch whose view leaves the
hierarchy: an upward drag cancelled itself. So:

- While a row is held, the other rows slide with `translateY`, set on the UI
  thread from the rows' frames at pick-up (`rowShifts` / `restackRows` in
  `reorder.ts`). Nothing renders as the row passes each place.
- On the drop, one render does everything: the ghost goes, the store gets the
  new order (so the rows really are in it) and the slides are gone. Each row
  is already where its layout now puts it, so nothing moves on screen.
- "The slides are gone" is done with the rows' keys, not by zeroing them. The
  slides live on the UI thread, and Reanimated re-applies them to every React
  commit, so a zero sent from JavaScript would land a frame or more after the
  new order: the rows would flash out of place. Instead each row's key is its
  task id plus an `epoch` that goes up with every drop that changed the order
  (`reorderRowKey`), so that render mounts the rows anew, and a row only
  follows slides made in its own epoch. A drop back in the same place, a
  cancelled drag and "Do this next" leave the rows mounted.
- The "Up next" look changes rows' heights, so it is not part of that render:
  it follows about 50 ms later, eased by a 180 ms layout transition.
- "Do this next" and Move up / Move down use the same 180 ms layout
  transition (the transition is put on the rows one render before the change,
  so it is in place for the layout pass). Not with Reduce Motion.

### Accessibility

Dragging is never needed. Each open row's checkbox has these VoiceOver
actions, shown only when they would do something:

- "Do this next" (not for the first open task)
- "Move up" (from the third open task on: for the second, one place up is
  "Do this next", so only that is offered)
- "Move down" (not for the last open task)

VoiceOver hears "<task> is next" when a task becomes the first open one, and
"Moved up: <task>" / "Moved down: <task>" otherwise. After a move its focus
is sent back to the moved row (the rows change places, which would otherwise
lose it). The handle itself is hidden from VoiceOver, and so is the dragged
row's copy. The one-time hint reads to VoiceOver as "To reorder, use a
task's actions: Do this next, Move up or Move down."

### Analytics

`task_reordered` with `source`:

- `"drag"`: a drop that changed the order.
- `"move"`: Move up / Move down, from VoiceOver or the menu.
- `"next"`: "Do this next", from the menu or VoiceOver.

No task text, as everywhere. A drop back in the same place sends nothing.
Neither does "Do this next" chosen from a menu that was opened before the
list changed underneath (a widget tick, midnight): when the task is already
next, done or gone, nothing is sent and nothing is announced.

## Nag mode

Owner's decisions: Plus-only and opt-in; the default reminder style stays
Calm (the task-aware reminders above). Personas: Mom, Dad, Partner, Coach,
Drill sergeant (1.4.1 added Dad, and renamed Spouse to Partner: the label

from any voice: no "I love you", no pet names, no hugs, kisses or hearts,
and no line that reads as the user's real wife, husband, father or mother
speaking. **Mom, Dad and Partner are the annoying nagging types**: they keep going on about it,
repeat themselves, count their reminders, sigh, and are passive-aggressive
and long-suffering ("I'm not mad, I'm just asking. Again.", "No, no, take

### How many, and when

`planNagTimes` in `nag-plan.ts`. A level sets the day's count: Gentle 4,
Firm 7, Relentless 11 (`NAG_LEVEL_COUNTS`).

```
base   = the latest of: the window's start, now + 20 min,
         60 min after the last nag that went off (yesterday's last, on a new day)
n      = min(the level's count, whole hours between base and the window's end)
slack  = (end - base) - 60 min x (n - 1)
J      = floor(1.5 x slack / n)        (slack itself when n is 1)
t(0)   = base + random(0..J)
t(i+1) = t(i) + 60 min + random(0..J)
each t(i) is held back to  end - 60 min x (n - 1 - i)  so the rest still fit
```

- Whole minutes. Every nag is inside the window and at least 60 minutes
  after the one before. The hold-back is rarely needed (on average the last
  nag lands three quarters of the way through the slack) and keeps both
  rules true when it is.
- `random` is a generator seeded by the day, a per-install salt (kept in the
  record from day to day) and the number of the draw, so the same day on the
  same phone always gives the same times, and two phones differ.
- The extra gap is not a fixed 0 to 50 minutes: it is sized to the room
  left (`J`). With the default window, planned before 8:40, that is 0 to 77
  minutes for Firm, 0 to 16 for Relentless (eleven only just fit in twelve
  hours) and 0 to 202 for Gentle, so four nags are spread over the day
  rather than over by early afternoon. A flat `J = 50` would be a one-line
  change: Gentle would then be done by about 14:30 and Relentless would
  usually get eight or nine of its eleven.
- With under an hour of window left there is no nag today.
- Ids are `daily-tasks:<yyyy-MM-dd>:nag:<HHmm>`: the scheduler's prefix (so
  `runPlan` manages them) and the day prefix the widget's last tick clears.
- **The night the clocks go back** an hour of the local clock happens twice,
  so two nags 60 minutes apart could share an HHmm id. The later one is
  moved a minute at a time until its id is free (ids of nags that already
  went off count), the ones after it are pushed along to stay 60 minutes
  apart, and one pushed past the window's end is dropped.

**Planning the day again.** The times are drawn once and kept in the record.
When the day is planned again because a task was added or reworded, the
persona changed or names were hidden or shown, slots that already went off
stay as history and the slots still to come keep their id, time and step;
only their words are written again. Another level or window draws the rest
of the day again (still 20 minutes on, and 60 minutes after the last one
that went off); the new steps carry on from the last nag that went off, so
they keep rising. A task ticked off or removed doesn't plan the day again.

Every slot stays in the record with its id, time and step, also one there
is no line for (its `text` is `""` and it isn't scheduled), so it is back
when there is a line again. No two nags of a day say the same words: when a
step's own line was already said (the day was drawn again with another
count), the next line after it that wasn't said is used (`unsaidLine`), and
an AI line already said is skipped. Never a line from before it: a day that
has got pushier doesn't go back to an opening line, so with nothing later
left that nag has no line and isn't scheduled.

**Another time zone.** The record keeps the clock it was planned for
(`zone`: minutes ahead of UTC at the day's noon, `nagZoneOffset`). When the
phone is in another zone on the same local date, the record doesn't apply
until `ensureNagDay` has drawn the rest of the day again for the new clock
(inside the window, 60 minutes after the last nag that went off); until
then the calm reminders are used. A record saved without `zone` is used as
it is.

### What a nag says

- Steps run 1..n through the day and the lines rise with them: the nag at
  step `s` says line `s` of the batch (`lineForStep`). With more lines than
  nags (a day planned with one, two or three nags left, for which four
  lines are written) the first nag takes the first line, the last nag the
  last, and the ones between are spread evenly:
  `index = round((s - 1) x (lines - 1) / max(1, n - 1))`. So one nag gets an
  opening line, two get an opening and a closing one, three an opening, a
  middle and a closing one; the day no longer starts on "I've asked nicely,
  more than once". The same for the AI's lines (four asked for, one used:
  the first). With fewer lines than nags the lines are spread over the day
  and the nags left over have no words and aren't scheduled (they stay in
  the record). That no longer happens with names hidden: every level has
  enough general lines for its full day.
- A step is the nag's place in the day, not an hour: a day planned at four
  in the afternoon starts with the opening lines. So no built-in line names
  a time of day, and the AI is told not to.
- The built-in lines fill the slots at once (`builtInNagLines`, seeded by
  the day and the salt). When the AI's batch arrives its lines replace the
  words of the slots still to come, by step; a slot the batch has no line
  for keeps its built-in one.
- **No affectionate AI line is ever said** (`isAffectionateNagLine` in
  `nag.ts`). The proxy refuses such lines, but a batch fetched before that
  rule can still be in the day's saved record on a phone (in `lines` and
  already written into the slots), and an older proxy may answer. So the
  phone has one short check of its own, for this only: "I love", "love
  you", luv, ily, "with love", "my love", adore, sweetheart, sweetie,
  honey, babe, baby, darling, dearest, hug, kiss, xoxo, "much love", "lots
  of love", "all my love", "love" said to someone (followed by a comma, a
  full stop, "!", "?" or the end of the line), "dear" as an address (",
  dear" or "dear,") and heart, hug or kissing emoji, as whole words (no bare
  "hon": it is a word in other languages). Figures of speech are blanked
  first ("I'd love to", "love the", "oh dear", "dear me", "baby steps"), and
  a declaration ("I love you", "we adore you", "love ya") is never excused
  by a task's own words. It runs in three places: `parseNagResponse`
  drops the line from a new answer; `withLines` (used by `applyAiNagLines`
  and by `planNagDay` when it puts a kept batch back) skips it, so that
  slot keeps its built-in line; and `nagPlanFromRecord` replaces a slot of
  an AI record that already holds one with the built-in line for the same
  step, as it does for a ticked task. The words of the open tasks are
  excused, as on the proxy ("Buy honey" can be named); a heart never is.
  It is not a second blocklist: everything else is the proxy's job.
- Title: the app's name, then the persona's label ("Three Today · Mom",
  "Three Today · Drill sergeant"; `nagTitle`), so a nag can't be taken for
  a message from the user's mother. Body: the line.
  `kind: "nag"`, `data: { taskId?, step }` (ids and numbers only).
- **A task ticked off or removed** (also from the widget, once the app next
  runs): the strategy points that slot at the next open task with the
  built-in line for the same step, keeping its time and id. It is the same
  line on every sync, and stays the same as the day's nags go off, so
  nothing is rescheduled twice. A slot still to come that is scheduled
  with put-in words notes them in the record (1.4.1, `sent`, by
  `nagRecordWithPlannedWords`, saved by `nagStrategy` / `nagModeStrategy`
  behind the day's runs); its own `text` and `taskId` are kept, so an
  undone tick brings the task's own nags back (and the note comes off).
  Once the slot has gone off, `sent` is what it said; see "Limits".
- **Never the same nag twice** (`nagPlanFromRecord`): a line put in that way
  is never one the record already has in any slot, gone off or still to
  come, or one given to an earlier slot of the same plan (after the day was
  drawn again, or names were hidden and shown, the step's own line can
  belong to another slot). The next built-in line not yet said is used
  instead; with none left the slot isn't scheduled. The same when names
  were just hidden and the record hasn't been planned again yet: the
  general lines go in exactly as `planNagDay` will write them.
- A task's name in a built-in line is cleaned, quoted and cut to 40
  characters, without the punctuation at its end (so never “Fix the bike!”.)
  and put in as typed (`$$` or `$&` in a task is not read as a pattern). The
  full stop of an initial that ends the name stays (“Email J.R.”).
- **How often a built-in nag names a task** (names shown, at least one open
  task): each third of the day draws its `{task}` lines first, hottest pool
  first, and a third with two or more nags keeps exactly one general line.
  A full day: Gentle 3 of 4, Firm 4 of 7, Relentless 8 of 11 name a task.
  The tasks take turns, so with two or three open each is named at least
  once.
- **Hide task names**: general built-in lines only, no `taskId`, and every
  level still gets its full count (Gentle 4, Firm 7, Relentless 11). Also for
  nags written before the switch was turned on: the strategy replaces their
  words at the next sync (the switch itself triggers one).

### What is sent, and when

`ensureNagDay` in `nag-day.ts` runs on a cold start, on coming to the
front, and when the tasks, the lock or a nag setting change. It only does
something when the day needs planning or the AI should be asked.

- **Sent:** the text of today's open tasks (up to three, 120 characters
  each), the persona, the level and how many lines are wanted, to
  `/api/momentum/nag-lines`, with the same anonymous Plus id as the other AI
  requests. Not the name, the goal, history or done tasks.
- `firstName` in `requestNagLines` is reserved: no caller passes it.
- **When:** once a day, the first time the app is open with nag mode on,
  permission granted, Plus confirmed, a task open and a nag still to come
  today. Once more that day at most, and only if a task was added or
  reworded, or the persona or level changed (`MAX_NAG_AI_CALLS_PER_DAY` =
  2; a failed request counts). Never while paused, in Calm, without Plus,
  or with "Hide task names" on.
- **Fallbacks:** offline, a timeout (20 s), a refusal (402), a bad answer,
  no proxy in the build: the built-in lines stay. Nothing is retried that
  day unless one of the changes above happens. Nothing blocks the UI and
  nothing throws; a storage failure means the calm reminders for now.
- **The record** (`daily-tasks/nag/v1` in AsyncStorage): `{ dayKey, persona,
  level, salt, tasksKey, lines, lineTasks, source, slots: [{ id, fireAtMs,
  step, text, taskId, sent? }], total, timingKey, draws, zone, hideNames,
  aiFetchedAt, aiCalls, aiFor, version }`. `tasksKey` is hashes of the open
  tasks' words. A new day makes a new record (the salt is kept).
- **Removing it.** The lines can name tasks. "Reset all data" removes the
  record entirely (`clearNagRecord`). Setting the style back to Calm takes
  every word out of it and keeps a stub (`stubNagRecord`, `nagRecordStub`;
  a saved Nag that is only waiting for Plus or permission keeps its
  record): the same `dayKey`, `salt`, `zone`, `aiCalls`, `aiFor` (the
  voice, the level and hashes of the tasks' words) and `aiFetchedAt`;
  `lines: []`, `lineTasks: []`, `tasksKey: ""`; the slots that already went
  off with `text: ""` and `taskId: null`; the slots still to come dropped.
  If the stub can't be saved the record is removed instead.
- **Calm and Nag again the same day** carries the day on from the stub:
  the rest of the day is drawn again with the level's count less the nags
  that already went off (a Relentless day never has more than 11 in all),
  never under 60 minutes after the last one that went off, and the day's
  two AI requests are not started again (the same tasks, voice and level
  aren't asked about twice; a new task, voice or level uses the second
  request if it is left). The words of the nags that went off before Calm
  are not kept, so a built-in line said in the morning can come round again
  in the afternoon after a Calm in between.
- **Runs and removals.** A request for lines can be in flight when the
  record is removed or emptied: before its answer is saved the record is
  read again, and the answer is thrown away when the record is gone or its
  fetch marker (`aiFetchedAt`, `aiFor`) isn't the request's. Each
  `ensureNagDay` run also notes, when it is queued (not when it starts),
  how many removals and emptyings there have been; if there has been one
  since, it saves and sends nothing. A run asked for after an emptying
  waits for it to finish, so its plan isn't overwritten by the stub.

### The plan

The store always syncs with `nagModeStrategy` (`nag-day.ts`):

- Not in nag mode: `taskAwareStrategy`, exactly as before.
- Nag mode: the calm plan's morning nudges (the next six mornings, and
  today's "nothing picked yet" ones while the day is empty) plus
  `nagStrategy`'s nags. The calm daytime and evening task nudges are not
  scheduled.
- Paused today: the mornings only.
- No record for today yet (the first sync of the day can beat the planning
  by a moment, or storage failed): the calm reminders, replaced as soon as
  the record is saved.
- All tasks done or none set: no nags (the record stays, for an untick).

Types: `planNagDay` takes a `NagDayPlanInput` (`nag-plan.ts`);
`ensureNagDay` takes a `NagDayInput` (`nag-day.ts`). They used to share the
one name.

`nagStrategy` reads the saved record: no network, and it never rejects
(anything wrong is an empty plan). It returns the future slots with the same
id, time and words, so an unchanged sync writes nothing. Its one write
(1.4.1): a slot still to come that it schedules with words other than its
own (a ticked or removed task, an affectionate stored AI line, a rude line
on a sensitive day) notes them in `sent`, and the note comes off when the
slot says its own words again. Only `sent` of slots still to come is
written: never a slot's `text` or `taskId`, never a slot that went off,
and no scheduled nag reads `sent` (only a slot that went off counts it, as
what it said), so a note that lands late can't change a pending nag. With
names hidden the put-in words are general lines. A record from 1.4.0 has
no `sent` and loads as it is; a stub keeps none. Not awaited by the sync; queued
behind `ensureNagDay`'s runs, the record read again first and saved only
if it is still the day and version the plan came from, and nothing saved
after a "Reset all data" or a Calm since it was asked for. The
version is kept (the plan is the one already scheduled), and nothing is
written when nothing changed.

The sync signature now also has Plus and the record's version, so the
reminders are written again when a nag setting changes, when the lines
arrive, and when Plus ends (the nags stop at that sync). `fitPlanToLimit`
still applies: at most 11 nags plus 6 mornings.

Only today's nags are ever pending. On a day the app isn't opened there are
none (the morning nudge written the day before still arrives).

### Focus, sound, and the button

- A nag is never `timeSensitive`: its interruption level is the default
  (`active`), so a Focus or Do Not Disturb holds it back. It is silent, like
  the other reminders. Only the focus timer's alert has a sound.
- Each nag has one button, **Stop for today** (category `three-today:nag`,
  registered at launch by `registerNotificationCategories`, which registers
  the focus timer's two categories and then the nag's, one after the other:
  expo-notifications reads the registered set, awaits, then writes it back
  with the new one, so two registrations in flight can wipe each other. All
  category registrations go through one queue in `notifications.ts`; each
  turn in the queue is given up on after 4 seconds, `CATEGORY_CALL_TIMEOUT_MS`,
  and the timeout resolves, so a native call that never settles can't hold
  up the focus timer's end notification: it is scheduled anyway, without
  its buttons at worst). It opens the app, like the focus timer's buttons
  (a background action is lost when the app isn't running), and pauses
  today's nags; a tap that launched the app is applied once the saved state
  has loaded. Tapping the nag itself opens the app as any reminder does.

### Pause

"Stop nagging me today" (Settings, or the button on a nag) saves today's
date in `nagPausedDate`. Today's pending nags are cancelled at the sync that
follows, no calm nudges take their place, and the AI isn't asked. Resume
clears it and the same nags (same times, same words) come back. The next
day the pause is over by itself.

### Limits

- Nags are planned when the app runs. A task ticked on the widget keeps its
  nags until the app next opens, unless it was the last open task (the
  widget then clears today's pending reminders, nags included).
- A morning nudge and the first nag can be under an hour apart: the hour
  rule is between nags.
- Raising the level or widening the window in the afternoon adds nags only
  where whole hours are left.
- The window can't cross midnight.
- "Never the same nag twice" holds for what the record knows. Since 1.4.1
  a slot scheduled with a line put in for a ticked task notes that line
  (`sent`) before its nag goes off, so a day drawn again later (another
  level or window) counts it as said and doesn't say it twice. The note is
  written when the slot is scheduled, not when it goes off: if the tick is
  undone and the note isn't taken off before the nag goes off (the app is
  closed first), the record counts the put-in line rather than the one
  said. A slot left with no line keeps no note (it isn't scheduled, so
  nothing was said). The write is best effort: if storage fails, or the
  app is closed before it lands, the next sync writes it, unless that nag
  has gone off by then (it then counts as having said its own words). Words said
  before a Calm aren't kept either (see "Calm and Nag again the same
  day").

### Analytics

No text, as everywhere: `nag_mode_enabled { persona, level }`,
`nag_mode_disabled`, `nag_paused_today { source: "settings" |
"notification" }`, `nag_lines_loaded { source: "ai" | "local", count }`
(each time the day is planned or the AI answers or fails). `persona` and
`level` were added to the allowlist; their values are the ids ("mom",
"firm").

### The server half

Nag mode is a Plus-only, opt-in reminder style: once a day the app gets a
batch of short lines about the open tasks in a persona's voice (Mom, Dad,
Partner, Coach, Drill sergeant) at a level (Gentle, Firm, Relentless) and schedules
them itself. This part is the proxy route, the app's client for it, and the
built-in lines.

- **Route:** `POST /api/momentum/nag-lines`, Plus-only, with the same secret,
  Plus check, limits and budgets as coach-note. Payload, response, safety
  rules and what leaves the phone are in `docs/momentum-ai-proxy.md` ("Nag
  lines").
- **Contract:** `server/providers/nag-contract.mjs` validates the payload,
  builds the prompt (task text is quoted data), and rebuilds the answer with
  `sanitizeNagLines`: bad shapes, bad indexes, long lines, links, phone-like
  numbers, hashtags, bidi marks and blocklist hits are dropped, steps are
  renumbered 1..n. Fewer than three lines left is a 502. The checks read a
  folded copy (NFKC, no combining marks, zero-width characters or soft
  hyphens); only a short list of emoji is kept, for mom, dad and spouse (no
  hearts); affection, romance, pet names and "your wife / mother" are on the
  blocklist for every voice (a few figures of speech are let through: "I'd
  love to", "oh dear", "baby steps"); the effort insults (lazy, maggot,
  slacker, pathetic and a few more: `NAG_DRILL_ALLOWED`) are blocked for
  every voice but the Drill sergeant, for whom "you are lazy / pathetic / a
  disgrace" and "pathetic little worm" are still dropped; contempt for who
  the person is ("waste of space", "what is wrong with you", "you are
  nothing", "I'll make you regret this") is blocked for every voice; and a
  task's own words can't excuse harm said to the person, "you are" with a
  blocked word, "it's your mother" or "I love you" (a bare "it's me" is
  fine). The prompt says not to mention the time of day or a meal,
  and a line that does is dropped (`NAG_CLOCK_WORDS` in the contract:
  morning, afternoon, evening, tonight, noon, midnight, breakfast, lunch,
  dinner, bedtime, and their plurals; the task's own words don't count, so
  "Book dinner" can be named). Bare "yourself" and "nobody" are no longer
  blocked ("Do yourself a favor", "Be kind to yourself"); "hurt yourself",
  "kill yourself" and "hang yourself" still are. A zero-width non-joiner or
  joiner between two Persian, Arabic or Indic letters (U+0600 to U+0DFF and
  the Arabic presentation forms) stays in the line that is shown, on the
  proxy and in the app; the checks read a copy without them, and anywhere
  else they are taken out.
- **Client:** `requestNagLines` in `lib/daily-tasks/nag.ts` returns
  `{ lines: NagLine[] }` (`{ step, taskIndex, text }`), with `taskIndex`
  pointing into the tasks it was given. It throws a `MomentumAiError` on any
  failure, after checking the answer again on the phone.
- **Built-in lines:** `builtInNagLines({ persona, level, tasks, count, seed, sensitiveDay })`
  for a failed call or no network. A sensitive task (`nagTaskIsSensitive`)
  is never named: it is left out of the tasks the lines take in turn, and
  with nothing else open the day has general lines only. With one among the
  day's open tasks (in `tasks`, or `sensitiveDay` from a caller that passes
  only some of them) the Drill sergeant's rude lines are left out of the
  pools. The lines put in later by `nagPlanFromRecord` (a ticked task, an
  affectionate stored AI line, names hidden or shown) follow the same two
  rules, and a slot saved before them that names such a task or is rude on
  such a day is replaced. Mom, Dad and Partner are written as the
  nagging types and no line of any voice is affectionate, intimate or
  claims a family history or a shared home ("I taught you to tie your
  shoes" and "our third housemate" are gone); the Drill sergeant's firm and
  relentless pools hold its rude lines (3 of 5 mid firm, 2 of 5 late firm, 4
  of 5 late relentless; `NAG_DRILL_RUDE` marks them), so a Gentle day has
  none, and a Firm day (7 nags) and a Relentless day (11) have about one in
  three (measured over 2,000 days: 29% and 36%), never none: when the draw
  gives a Firm day no rude line, one line of its last third is exchanged for
  a rude one. Tests check all of that. 34 lines per persona, sorted by stage
  (early, mid, late: the step's place in the day, not the hour) and by push
  (gentle, firm, relentless). The day runs early, mid, late in thirds.
  Gentle only ever uses the gentle lines; Firm adds the firm ones from the
  middle of the day; Relentless adds the dramatic ones at the end. The same
  seed gives the same day (use the date). No line repeats. With a task to
  name, each third names one in all its lines but one general line (none in
  a third of one nag): 3 of Gentle's 4, 4 of Firm's 7, 8 of Relentless's 11.
  `{task}` is the
  task's name, cleaned, quoted and cut to 40 characters, so every line is
  110 characters or fewer. 19 of the 34 are general (no `{task}`): early 6,
  mid 3 gentle and 3 firm, late 2 gentle, 3 firm and 2 relentless, so with
  names hidden every level has its full count and the lines differ from day
  to day. No line has a clock word (`NAG_CLOCK_WORDS`: morning, afternoon,
  evening, tonight, night, late, early, sunrise, sunset, lunch, dinner,
  bedtime and a few more) or poses as a real message ("it's your mother",
  "it's me", "call me"); tests check both, and that every line passes the
  proxy's sanitiser.
- **Labels:** `NAG_PERSONAS`, `NAG_LEVELS`, `NAG_PERSONA_LABELS` and
  `NAG_LEVEL_LABELS` in `lib/daily-tasks/nag.ts`.
- **Privacy:** the route accepts the open tasks' text, an optional first
  name (reserved), the persona and the level. The app sends no name (see "What is sent,
  and when"). The proxy logs none of it. `docs/privacy-policy.md` has the
  line; the published copy has to be updated with it.
- **Deploy:** the Worker needs `cd server && npx wrangler@4 deploy` after
  merge. Until then the app's call fails and it uses the built-in lines.

## What's new sheet

A one-time sheet that introduces a release's headline feature to people who
update into it. In 1.4 it is "Meet Nag mode". Owner's decision: highlight Nag
mode when someone updates, with what it does and the choices it has.

Files: `lib/daily-tasks/whats-new.ts` (pure: the announcements, who sees
what, when it may show, the sheet's words, the preview's lines),
`hooks/use-whats-new.ts` (when Today shows it),
`lib/daily-tasks/sheet-swap.ts` (`SHEET_SWAP_DELAY_MS`),
`lib/daily-tasks/action-sheet.ts` (the app's iOS menus, so it is known when
one is open), `components/daily-tasks/nag-intro-sheet.tsx` (the
sheet), `components/daily-tasks/nag-intro.tsx` (the sheet wired to the store,
the paywall and the turn-on flow), `hooks/use-nag-turn-on.ts` (turning Nag
on, shared with Settings), `lib/daily-tasks/nag-copy.ts` (words shared with
Settings).

### Who sees it

Saved state: `whatsNewSeen: string[]`, the ids of announcements already shown
or not needed (normalised in `storage.ts`; anything missing or damaged loads
as an empty list).

`whatsNewDecision(state)`:

- First run not finished (`hasSeenOnboarding` false): nothing.
- Otherwise the oldest announcement whose id is not in `whatsNewSeen`.
- Except one whose feature is already in use (`alreadyUsing`): it is marked
  seen without being shown. For `1.4-nag` that is a saved Nag style
  (`notifications.style === "nag"`, with or without Plus right now).

So:

| Who | Saved state when 1.4 first opens | Sees it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used the app before 1.4 | onboarding finished, no `whatsNewSeen` | yes, once |
| New install on 1.4 | none; first run marks every current id seen when it finishes (`markOnboardingSeen`, and `completeMomentumOnboarding` when that is what ends onboarding) | no |
| Was half-way through first run when updating | onboarding not finished | no (it finishes as a new install does) |
| "Reset all data" | a fresh state, then first run again | no |
| Already has Nag as the saved style | `style: "nag"` | no; marked seen at once |
| Has seen it | `whatsNewSeen` has the id | no |

A new install on a later release still sees that release's announcement: only
the ids that exist when first run finishes are marked.

### When

Only in the quiet window right after a launch or a return to the
foreground, so it never slides up in the middle of planning.

A foreground session starts when the saved state has loaded and the app is
in front (a launch, or coming back from the background), and ends when the
app goes to the background. Each session is one chance:

- If Today is idle, and stays idle for `WHATS_NEW_DELAY_MS`, the sheet
  shows. The delay is `SHEET_SWAP_DELAY_MS + 150` (800 ms): longer than a
  sheet takes to animate away, so it is never presented while another is
  still closing.
- If the user does anything first, it gives up for this session and tries
  again at the next foreground or launch. It does not come back when the
  keyboard goes down, when the rollover card is answered or when a sheet
  closes.

Idle (`todayIdleForWhatsNew`) means all of:

- Today's tab is the one showing;
- first run isn't up; no paywall, Ideas, brain dump, routines or focus
  screen is open; no celebration is showing;
- no focus session exists (running, paused or at its time's-up check-in);
- yesterday's leftovers (the rollover card) are not waiting;
- the keyboard is down, no row is being dragged and no break-down is on
  its way.

"The user did something" is any of those turning true, a task being added,
removed, edited, moved or ticked (`whatsNewActivity`), or, as the delay
ends, a menu being open or a text field having focus (a hardware keyboard
shows no keyboard). If Today is not idle as the session starts (the
rollover card is up, another tab is showing), that session's chance is gone
too.

Three things only need a moment at launch and do not end the session's
chance:

- whether the user has Plus is still being checked (the sheet's Plus line
  and its button depend on it): the delay starts once it is known;
- a rating ask was just made, or the aha paywall's timer is running: as the
  delay ends it asks again every `WHATS_NEW_RETRY_MS` (2 s);
- iOS has not made the app active yet: the same retry.

The check as the delay ends is `whatsNewMoment`: "yes", "later" (the retry)
or "no" (not in this session).

**If iOS drops it.** iOS presents one thing at a time and drops a sheet
presented over something else (a menu, an alert, a sheet still closing)
without saying so. Three guards:

- the delay is longer than `SHEET_SWAP_DELAY_MS`;
- the app's iOS menus (a task's menu, the timer lengths) open through
  `showActionSheet`, and an open one counts as the user being busy;
- a watchdog: if the sheet's `onShow` has not fired
  `WHATS_NEW_SHOW_TIMEOUT_MS` (1.5 s) after it was asked to open, it is
  taken back **without** being marked seen, so Today is not left counting
  as busy, and it is asked again 2 s later. After `WHATS_NEW_MAX_TRIES` (3)
  in one session it waits for the next foreground.

While the sheet is up it counts as "something on screen" for the rating ask,
the aha paywall and the win-back paywall, so none of them comes up over it.
The same holds for the moment between the sheet closing and the paywall or
the focus screen opening (below).

It is marked seen when the user closes it, however they do: "Not now", the
close button, a swipe down, Android's back button, "See Plus" (the sheet
closes for the paywall), or Nag turning on. If the app is quit while it is
still up it was not closed, so it shows at a later launch.

**A focus prompt while it is up** (a tap on the Live Activity or the end
notification, "start my next task" from the widget or Siri with another
timer on): the sheet closes first, **not** marked seen because the user did
not close it, and the focus screen opens `SHEET_SWAP_DELAY_MS` later, so two
modals are never presented at once. The sheet comes back at a later
foreground.

When the user closes it, VoiceOver's focus goes back to Today's title once
it has gone. Not when it closes for the paywall or the focus screen: the
focus is theirs to take.

### Try Nag mode / See Plus

One implementation with Settings' Reminder style choice: `useNagTurnOn`
(`requestNag`), with the voice and level picked on the sheet.

One question at a time: while iOS is being asked for notifications, or
while "Turn on Nag mode?" is up, another tap does nothing (a double tap
used to queue two dialogs). This holds in Settings too.

- **With Plus:** iOS is asked for notifications if it hasn't been; refused
  shows "Notifications unavailable" and nothing turns on. Then the "Turn on
  Nag mode?" question (the only place the AI service is mentioned), asked
  once per install. The sheet stays up under both. "Turn on" saves the
  voice, the level and the style with one call (`setReminderStyle("nag", {
  persona, level })`, which sends three actions one after the other: voice,
  level, style), closes the sheet, and Today shows "Nag mode is on.
  Change it in Settings › Reminders." "Not now" on the question leaves the
  sheet as it was.
- **Without Plus ("See Plus"):** the sheet closes, then the paywall opens
  through the usual gate (source `nag_mode`, `plus_gate_hit { feature:
  "nag_mode" }`). In between Today still counts as busy, so no rating ask
  or other paywall slips in, and VoiceOver's focus is not sent to Today's
  title.
  If Plus is bought there, the same steps follow once the paywall has
  closed, with the voice and level that were picked, and the same line
  shows on Today. Closed without buying: nothing more.

### Seeing it again

Settings › Reminders has a row under the style card, "What is Nag mode?",

the same sheet with the same buttons. It never changes `whatsNewSeen`, and
the sheet never comes up by itself in Settings.

### Analytics

Ids only, no text: `whats_new_shown { id, source }` and `whats_new_action {
id, action: "try" | "dismiss", persona, level, source }`. `id` is the

### Adding the next announcement

1. Add an entry to `RELEASED` in `lib/daily-tasks/whats-new.ts`: `{ id:
   "1.5-<feature>" }` (letters, digits, dots, dashes, underscores; 40
   characters at most), with `alreadyUsing` if someone can have the feature on already.
   Keep the old entries: they are what a finished first run marks as seen.
2. Build its sheet and show it on Today where the Nag one is
   (`whatsNew.openId === "<id>"` in `app/(tabs)/index.tsx`), closing with
   `closeWhatsNew` and passing `whatsNew.shown` to its `onShow` (without
   it the watchdog takes the sheet back after 1.5 s). Send
   `whats_new_shown` and `whats_new_action` with its id.
3. Add its cases to `tests/whats-new.test.ts`. The UI test setup turns
   announcements off for every other suite (`__setAnnouncementsForTests([])`
   in `tests/ui/setup.ts`), so only the what's-new suites see them.

One announcement shows at a time, oldest first, and at most one in a
foreground session. Someone who skipped a release would get the older one,
and the next at a later foreground: when there are two, decide whether the
older one should still be shown.

### Limits

- The watchdog trusts the Modal's `onShow`. If one of the app's alerts (for
  example "Set today's three?") is opened in the 800 ms before the sheet
  comes up, iOS holds the sheet back; it is taken back after 1.5 s and
  tried again, up to three times in the session.
- `whats_new_shown` is sent when the sheet is asked to open, so a dropped
  try counts as shown.
- Someone who always does something in the first 800 ms of every session
  (answers the rollover card, starts typing) does not see it until a
  session where they don't.
- A task ticked from the widget just before the app is opened can be read
  as activity once the app is in front, and cost that session's chance.
